If you sell on WhatsApp, you know the loop. A customer says "I want it". You send your account number. They say "I've sent it". You open your bank app, scroll, and check. Sometimes a screenshot arrives that you can't confirm for an hour.

None of that is your product. It is admin, and it eats the hours you could spend selling. Here is a routine that cuts most of it.

## 1. Agree the price before the money talk

Many payment chases start with a misunderstanding: delivery fee, size, colour, quantity. Put the full order in one place before anyone pays. When the customer has chosen items themselves from a shop link, the order is written down, so there is nothing to argue about later.

## 2. Send one payment link, not an account number

An account number invites mistakes: wrong digits, wrong amount, a transfer that arrives with no name on it. A payment link carries the exact amount, and the payment is tied to one specific order.

## 3. Don't pack until it is paid

This is the hard one, because you want to be helpful and fast. But packing first means you carry the risk. A short, friendly line works: "I'll pack it as soon as the payment shows."

## 4. Let the confirmation come from the payment, not a screenshot

A screenshot is a picture, and pictures can be edited or old. Confirmation that comes from the payment provider can't be. You can pack and send without second-guessing.

## 5. Update the customer once, automatically

Most "any update?" messages happen because the customer is in the dark. If they are told when payment is received and when the order is on its way, they ask less.

## Try it for a week

Pick one routine and use it for every order for a week: link first, pack after payment. Count how many "have you checked?" messages you stop sending. That number tells you what you got back.

Gbam is built around this routine: shop link, payment link, confirmation from Paystack and automatic WhatsApp updates. But the habit works with any tool, and the habit is what matters.
